A PC remote for apps, shortcuts and everyday tasks

Baroham turns app launches, shortcuts and file opening into buttons on a screen. Give an unused Android phone or older tablet a place on your desk as a PC control panel.

Keep apps and shortcuts close

Put mail, your browser, project folders, copy and paste on named buttons. A spare tablet gives you room for larger controls.

Make repetitive tasks a macro

You can open a search website and enter a frequently used phrase with one button. Combine shortcuts, text and delays in the order you need.

Bring back your workspace

Try assigning workspace restore to a long press. Hold the device screen for half a second to open your saved apps and restore their window layout.

Use clocks and widgets between tasks

Set an idle interval to show a clock or Neko cat widget. Optional news, aquarium and campfire widgets can give your desk a different mood.

What do you need to reuse an old Android phone?

  • A Windows PC: Run Studio on Windows 10 1809 or later, or Windows 11, on x64.
  • A phone or tablet: Android 5.0 or later is supported. Phones and tablets use the same Android app.
  • A connection: Use Wi-Fi on the same local network or a USB data cable. Your PC can use wired Ethernet.

For example, a Galaxy S8 (Android 9) can become a compact shortcut panel, while an LG G Pad 8.0 (Android 5.0.2) offers a larger button and widget screen. USB compatibility varies by device and cable.

Turn an old tablet or phone into a macro pad

  1. Install Baroham Studio on your PC

    Download the Windows installer and open Studio.

  2. Install Baroham on the Android device

    Start the Android phone connection guide in Studio’s Devices menu. With the PC and phone on the same network, scan the QR code to download the app in your browser. Follow the installation instructions.

  3. Connect over Wi-Fi or USB

    Wi-Fi on the same network is a simple place to start. Enter the six-digit code shown in Studio on your phone. For USB, follow the connection guidance below.

  4. Create your buttons

    Select a button in Studio’s preview and choose an app, shortcut, file, folder, URL or macro. Save and apply updates the connected phone’s screen too.

Should you use Wi-Fi or USB?

Wi-Fi · An easy starting point

Use the phone and PC on the same local network. No SIM card or mobile plan is needed. Saved pairing details let the device reconnect later.

USB · When Wi-Fi is unavailable

After installing the app, USB AOA works without shared Wi-Fi. You need a data cable and accessory approval on the phone. Windows may require a separate UsbDk driver installation. Normal use does not require USB debugging.

If an older device has trouble with USB, try Wi-Fi first. Some tablets need the cable disconnected and reconnected after an app update. Troubleshoot a connection →

Common questions about repurposing old phones

Can I use a phone without a SIM card?

Yes. Baroham connects to your PC over Wi-Fi or USB, so no SIM card, phone number or mobile plan is required.

Can I use a tablet that no longer gets manufacturer updates?

You can use an Android 5.0+ device that can install and run the app. USB compatibility varies, so Wi-Fi is a useful starting point.

Can I use Baroham as a free Stream Deck alternative?

For app launching, shortcuts, macros and media controls, you can put those actions on touch buttons. Build a PC control panel with a phone or tablet you already own instead of a dedicated keypad.

Does it work when my PC is off?

Baroham’s button screens and widgets connect to Studio running on your PC. Normal communication and control cannot continue when the PC is shut down or fully asleep.

Does it work without internet access?

After installation and setup, you can connect over USB or a local network that allows communication between devices. Local app launches and shortcuts do not need internet access. Websites, news, weather and other online features do.

Can I start without a phone?

Yes. Use the virtual Baroham window with a mouse, or place it on a small touch monitor or second display. Explore virtual Baroham →
